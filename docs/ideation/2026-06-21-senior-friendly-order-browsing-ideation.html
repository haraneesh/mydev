Ideation

Senior-Friendly Order Browsing

Ranked product ideas for simplifying Suvai's order/product browsing flow for shoppers who are 60+ or less technically confident, while preserving the current working checkout path.

Date: 2026-06-21 Topic: senior-friendly-order-browsing Focus: simplify product browsing and improve icon labels Mode: repo-grounded

Grounding Context

Codebase context. The order flow is a Meteor/React app. The home page links to ordering via /cart or /neworder, product selection is rendered through ProductsOrderMain and ProductsOrderMobile, product cards through ProductForNonAdmin, cart review through CartDetails, and registered-phone checkout through GetUserPhoneNumber.

Observed usability test. On a mobile-sized viewport, ORDER NOW worked, /neworder loaded a long category/product surface, adding one item updated the cart count, checkout led to a cart review, and submitting with the provided test mobile number reached the success page. The final URL was /order/success/undefined, which is outside this ideation scope but worth fixing separately.

Direct code evidence. Header controls are icon-only buttons for profile, cart, and menu in imports/ui/components/AuthenticatedNavigation/Menu.js:20, :30, and :50. The authenticated toolbar has hidden text labels with display: 'none' in imports/ui/components/ToolBar/ToolBar.js:16 and icon buttons at :97, :109, :136, and :147. Product browsing renders a vertical category rail with many categories from ProductsOrderMobile.js:266 onward, subcategory pills at :164-200, and individual product cards. Product cards turn an added item into a quantity selector in ProductForNonAdmin.js:198-220. The registered mobile number modal appears only after cart placement in GetUserPhoneNumber.js:48-84.

External context. NN/g's research on older adults notes that interfaces often fail by using small text, poor contrast, and tiny targets, and that small targets and tiny mobile text are especially difficult for older users. WCAG 2.2 requires UI component names and roles to be programmatically determined, and defines status messages so success, waiting, and error changes can be exposed to assistive technologies.

Sources. NN/g: Usability for Older Adults; WCAG 2.2: Name, Role, Value; WCAG 2.2: Status Messages.

1

Senior Mode Order Layout

Browse density Confidence 82% Complexity Medium
Description Add a persistent, user-selectable "Easy ordering" mode for the order page: larger type, fewer columns, a collapsed category drawer, more whitespace, and always-visible cart summary. It should not replace the existing dense layout; it should let less confident shoppers choose a calmer version once and keep that preference.
Basis direct: The mobile order UI currently forces a sticky category rail with many categories in ProductsOrderMobile.js:266-360, while product cards are dense and often uppercase. external: NN/g identifies small type, poor contrast, and tiny targets as major barriers for older adults.
Rationale This tackles the highest-friction surface observed in testing without removing power-user efficiency. It also gives the team a single design target for future senior-friendly work instead of one-off tweaks.
Downsides Requires layout branching and QA across category navigation, search, cart footer, and product cards. It can drift unless shared components are used.
2

Plain-Language Icon System

Accessible semantics Confidence 91% Complexity Low
Description Create a small accessible icon-button wrapper used by header, toolbar, product quantity, and cart controls. Every icon button gets an explicit accessible name, visible text where space allows, a minimum touch target, and a consistent badge pattern for counts.
Basis direct: The header renders icon-only buttons around person, local_mall, and menu in Menu.js:20-57. Toolbar labels exist but are hidden with display: 'none' in ToolBar.js:16-20. The usability test text output exposed strings like personlocal_mallmenu and addADD ITEMS. external: WCAG 2.2 says UI component name and role must be programmatically determinable.
Rationale This is the fastest high-confidence improvement. It helps screen reader users, voice control users, and anyone who does not recognize the icon set.
Downsides Needs careful visual styling to avoid crowding the already-tight mobile header. Some labels may need visually-hidden text rather than visible text.
3

Guided Category Landing

Navigation clarity Confidence 78% Complexity Medium
Description Replace the immediate long category rail with a short landing panel: "What are you shopping for today?" with 4-6 common entry points such as Fresh vegetables, Daily staples, Breakfast, Protein rich, Gut health, and Search. The full category list remains available behind "All categories."
Basis direct: The product page starts directly inside Tab.Container defaulting to the configured product category at ProductsOrderMobile.js:260, then renders every category nav item. The test landed on Millets with 32 add buttons visible in the DOM, even for a shopper who may only want a familiar grocery path.
Rationale Older and less technical shoppers benefit from recognition over exploration. A guided starting point reduces the cognitive work of interpreting a taxonomy.
Downsides Requires deciding and maintaining the common paths. If too promotional, it could hide the category users actually need.
4

Compact Product Cards With Confirmed Add State

Product card decision load Confidence 80% Complexity Medium
Description After "Add to Cart," keep the card compact and show a clear added state: "Added: 150 g" with two large buttons, "Change quantity" and "Remove." Open the full quantity selector only when the user asks to change it.
Basis direct: AddToCart swaps the button for a full quantity selector and returnable controls in ProductForNonAdmin.js:198-220. During testing, the first card expanded into many visible quantities: 150 g, 200 g, 500 g, 1 kg, 1.5 kg, 2 kg, plus delete.
Rationale The current behavior confirms the add, but it also adds visual noise exactly where the user just succeeded. A compact state preserves confidence while keeping browsing calm.
Downsides Adds one tap for users who routinely adjust quantities immediately. Needs careful handling for discounted quantity options.
5

Checkout Readiness Banner

Checkout expectation Confidence 74% Complexity Low
Description On the product and cart pages, show a small, plain-language checkout readiness line: "You can place this order with your registered mobile number. New to Suvai? Sign up first." If the user is already logged in, replace it with "You are ordering as [name]."
Basis direct: The registered-phone modal is only introduced after pressing Place Order in GetUserPhoneNumber.js:48-84. The usability test made it through, but the requirement appears at the final commitment moment.
Rationale This prevents surprise at checkout and supports less technical users who may not distinguish between browsing, cart review, login, and order placement.
Downsides Adds another message to already-busy pages. It must stay short and avoid sounding like an error.
6

Assisted Search First

Navigation clarity Confidence 68% Complexity Medium
Description Promote search from a passive field into an assisted shopper: large search input, suggested terms, typo-tolerant matching, and "Did you mean?" recovery for common spellings and transliterations. Keep categories as secondary navigation.
Basis direct: Product search exists at the top of the order card through ProductsOrderMain.js:215-223, but the page still emphasizes category browsing. external: NN/g reports older users are often punished by unforgiving inputs and simple typos.
Rationale Grocery shoppers often know what they want by name. Assisted search can bypass taxonomy learning, especially with mixed English/Tamil product names and spelling variation.
Downsides Requires search quality work and product-name synonym data. Bad suggestions could reduce trust quickly.
7

Accessible Order Progress Announcements

Accessible semantics Confidence 70% Complexity Low
Description Add accessible status regions for add-to-cart, cart count updates, checkout modal validation, and final order success. Pair visual toast/count changes with screen-reader-friendly messages such as "Barnyard flakes added. Cart total is Rs. 51."
Basis direct: The cart count visually updated during testing, but the accessible text surface was awkward and console warnings showed prop/DOM issues. external: WCAG 2.2 defines status messages as changes that can be presented by assistive technologies without taking focus.
Rationale This turns invisible state changes into explicit feedback. Older users who rely on zoom, screen readers, or simply slower visual scanning get a stronger sense of "what just happened."
Downsides Requires discipline to avoid noisy announcements. Needs testing with actual assistive technology.

Rejection Summary

#IdeaReason Rejected
1Full redesign of checkout and paymentScope overrun: the requested focus is product browsing and icon clarity, not the whole order/payment system.
2Remove all categories and use only searchToo risky: categories are useful for browsing groceries and existing users may depend on them.
3Voice orderingInteresting but high complexity and not grounded enough in the tested pain; better as a later brainstorm variant.
4Desktop-only senior ordering pageContradicts the observed mobile use case and NN/g notes older adults increasingly use smartphones.
5Make every icon text-onlyDuplicates the stronger icon-system idea but loses compactness and visual scanning benefits.
6Increase every product image sizeBelow ambition floor: may help recognition, but worsens list length and does not address navigation or semantics.
7Hide health categories for senior usersUnjustified assumption: health categories may be especially useful to this audience.
